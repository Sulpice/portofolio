<p>
I am a Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural and International Economics at <a href="https://www.fsaa.ulaval.ca/faculte/departement-deconomie-agroalimentaire-et-des-sciences-de-la-consommation" target="_blank">Université Laval</a>, advised by Lota D. Tamini and Bruno Larue. My research lies in international trade, macroeconomics, and applied econometrics, with a focus on climate change and agri-food markets.
</p>

<p><b>I am on the 2026–2027 job market.</b></p>

<p>
In my <a href="{{ 'assets/pdf/Chapter3_Final_version.pdf' | relative_url }}" target="_blank">job market paper</a>, I study how consumption patterns and international trade shape the unequal welfare consequences of climate change, using a multi-country Ricardian trade model with non-homothetic preferences.
</p>

<p>
Before and alongside my Ph.D., I worked at the <a href="https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/poverty" target="_blank">World Bank</a>, <a href="https://agriculture.canada.ca/en" target="_blank">Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada</a>, and the <a href="https://www.bceao.int" target="_blank">Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO)</a>.
</p>
